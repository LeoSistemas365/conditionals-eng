Conditional 1 Real & Probable

The First Conditional

Used for realistic situations, future possibilities, plans, promises, or warnings that are very likely to happen.

Formula Breakdown
If + Present Simple + Will + Base Verb
  • Future Prediction: If it rains tomorrow, I will stay at home.
  • Promises/Plans: If you help me study, I will buy you coffee.
Conditional 2 Hypothetical & Unreal

The Second Conditional

Used for imaginary, hypothetical, or impossible situations in the present/future, as well as giving advice.

Formula Breakdown
If + Past Simple + Would + Base Verb
  • Imaginary Outcome: If I won the lottery, I would buy a yacht.
  • Advice (If I were you): If I were you, I would take the job offer.

Pro Tip: "Was" vs "Were" in 2nd Conditional

In formal English and academic tests, always use were instead of was for all subjects in the if-clause (e.g., "If I were rich..." or "If she were here...").
